Total variation reads as decisions. KL does not.

Compression reports summarize how far a compressed model moved from the dense one, usually by KL divergence. A deployment that relies on the dense model's outputs needs something else: how many of its decisions changed. The rate at which the arg-max token changes tracks total variation at a median ratio of 1.05, with no fitted constant.

The headline result

The same 802 configurations, measured two ways

Against total variation, the flip rate falls on the identity line. Against √KL it follows a power law whose level depends on the corpus: on GSM8K, where a few tokens carry the divergence, the same KL means fewer changed decisions than on natural text.

Contributions

Four findings

Measured under teacher forcing: at the same positions and on the same prefixes, the divergence between a dense model and a perturbed copy, and how often the arg-max token changes.

01

Total variation reads as decisions

Flips per unit of total variation have a median of 1.05 across nine families, with no fitted constant. An isotropic small-noise limit that depends only on the dense distributions explains why the value is near one.

0.89–1.29range covering 95% of configurations
02

KL reads as decisions only within a corpus

Within one reference (a model on a corpus), nine families share one square-root curve. Across models and corpora its coefficient moves fourfold, so KL misorders compressors compared across reports eight times as often as total variation.

1.6–2.6×shift across corpora of a single model
03

Why: KL averages before it takes the root

An exact decomposition attributes most of KL's corpus dependence to averaging over tokens (a Jensen factor). Statistics that are first order at every token, such as Hellinger distance, share total variation's stability. Reports rarely give them.

1.33× · 1.54× · 4.0×spread across references: TV, Hellinger, √KL
04

It predicts greedy speculative decoding

Total variation measured under teacher forcing predicts a draft's greedy agreement without task-specific calibration. KL must be calibrated on the task and still has two to three and a half times the error.

1.1–2.4%mean error on acceptance in vLLM
Mechanism

Why a square root

A small perturbation moves every logit by a small amount ε. The gap between the two leading tokens, which decides whether the arg-max changes, moves in proportion to ε. KL is smallest when nothing changes, so it can only grow as ε². Comparing a quantity that grows like ε with one that grows like ε² gives a square root.

KL ≈ ½ Varp(Δz)  ·  second order
𝔼|Δg| = A·√(2 KL)  ·  first order
flips ≈ ρ0 𝔼|Δg| / 2,   κ ≈ ρ0 A / √2

Here ρ0 is the density of top-2 margins at zero and A is an anisotropy factor. The exponent ½ follows from the orders. What has to be measured is whether A is comparable across perturbations.

  • A is nearly constant within a reference (median coefficient of variation 2.6%).
  • Every structured compressor's A lies within 0.915–1.005 of isotropic Gaussian noise of the same Fisher norm.
  • Compressors push margins down as often as up: the share of negative shifts has median 0.504 over 372 configurations.
Regime

Where the relation bends

flips/√KL is flat up to about 0.25 nats, which is 8 down to 3 bits on the rounding ladder and most 4-bit models. Above that point, higher-order terms enter and the square root increasingly underestimates flips. At 2-bit weights, where 94–100% of decisions flip, no power law in KL can continue.

  • 10⁻³ to 0.25 nats: both statistics behave as described.
  • Up to about 2 nats: √KL underestimates flips (1.21× the window value above 1 nat), while TV still reads them.
  • At 2 bits: flips per unit of TV stay at 0.75–0.98 of their window value. Only TV stays within 25%.

After a Holm correction over the nine families, only layer removal remains significant, which matches its lower anisotropy factor. In the window where all families coexist, eight of them are within 1.6%.

Out of sample

Two pre-registered tests mark the limits

Predictions were registered before the data were generated, and each corpus was scored once. Total variation kept its near-unit ratio. KL kept its square-root law but lost its conversion factor and its shared family curve.

Held-out code corpus (MBPP), 8 models

The exponent (0.48–0.57) and the flips/TV ratio (0.98–1.05) held for all eight models. The shared family curve in KL failed its registered test for all eight.

Mixture of experts and QAT

Application

Speculative decoding, the exact-match consumer

A speculative decoder accepts a draft token only when it matches the target, so it prices decision fidelity directly. We ran 23 compressed self-drafts and 20 cross-model drafts over ten targets, then served two Qwen3 targets in vLLM with real kernels on GSM8K and MBPP prompts that were disjoint from the calibration text.

Neither statistic reliably picks the fastest draft: quantized kernels do not cost in proportion to their bytes, so the choice also needs a cost model of the kernels.

Practice

What to report, and when

Both divergences come from the same teacher-forced pass, so neither is cheaper to compute. The choice depends on who reads the number.

Exact agreement

Speculative drafts, cached outputs, regression tests

Report the flip rate itself on the task prompts. To screen drafts, use total variation, which predicted acceptance in vLLM within 1.1–2.4%.

Across corpora or reports

Report total variation, per corpus

KL misorders compressors in 8.1% of pairs across corpora of one model and in 10.8% across models and corpora. Total variation does so in 0.7% and 1.3%.

Within one corpus

KL ranks methods correctly here

With one corpus and one dense reference, KL misorders only 0.2% of pairs. Adding total variation next to it gives the scale in decisions.

Scope

What these statistics do not measure

Decisions ≠ accuracy

Fidelity to the dense model is not benchmark quality

On Gemma-3-4B, at a matched GSM8K divergence of 0.10 nats and nearly the same flip rate, magnitude pruning costs 33.3 accuracy points while Wanda gains 4.8. Per-position statistics also do not say how changed decisions compound over free generation.

Measured window

10⁻³ to about 2 nats · 8 to 3 bits · 1B–72B

Results cover dense decoder-only transformers with post-training changes to fixed weights or to the KV cache, greedy decoding, teacher forcing, five corpora in two languages and one held-out code corpus. MoE and QAT enter only as a post hoc extension. Fine-tuning, distillation and multimodal inputs are untested.

Reproduce

Measure your own dense–compressed pair

The released code reports all three statistics (total variation, flips and KL) for any dense model and compressed checkpoint that share a tokenizer. It flags a flips/TV ratio outside the central 95% of the published configurations (0.89–1.29).

01

Install

Clone the repository and install the glod package.

$git clone https://github.com/beatrizalmeidaf/elastic_weight_streaming && cd elastic_weight_streaming && pip install -e .
02

Report a pair

Writes TV, flips and KL per corpus, flips/TV, κ, the Jensen factor J and speculative-acceptance estimates, as JSON and Markdown.

$python -m glod report --dense Qwen/Qwen3-4B --compressed cfg:gptq4 --out report/
03

Reproduce the grid

Builds the reference corpus and scores every perturbation family by teacher forcing. The Makefile also has Slurm and Docker targets.

$make corpus grid MODEL=Qwen/Qwen3-4B DEVICE=cuda:0
FAQ

Common questions

Is GLOD an accuracy benchmark?
No. GLOD measures fidelity to the dense model: how many next-token decisions change under teacher forcing. The "damage" in its name means distributional deviation, not lost capability. Two compressors with the same flip rate can differ by tens of accuracy points.
Is total variation cheaper to compute than running the models?
No. KL and total variation come from the same teacher-forced pass over both models. If you can run both models on the task, count flips directly. Total variation matters when you read a reported number instead of measuring one yourself, for example when you compare results across papers, leaderboards or corpora.
Then why not just report KL?
Within one corpus and one dense reference, KL ranks compressors correctly (0.2% of pairs misordered). Across corpora or models, its conversion into decisions moves fourfold, because KL averages over tokens before the square root is taken. Of two compressors whose flip rates differ by at least 10%, KL gives the smaller divergence to the one that changes more decisions in 11% of cases. Total variation does so in 1%.
Does it hold at very low bit widths?
The square-root relation for KL is flat up to about 0.25 nats and breaks at 2-bit weights, where 94–100% of decisions flip. Flips per unit of total variation stay within 25% of their window value throughout.
How are the divergences computed?
Over the top-64 tokens of the dense distribution plus an aggregated tail. Over the full vocabulary, KL and TV are a median 1.003 and 1.001 times larger. bf16 logits create exact top-1/top-2 ties, so every analysis of margins uses references rescored with an fp32 output head.
Which models were studied?
19 checkpoints from six families: Gemma-3 (1B, 4B, 12B, 27B), Qwen3 (4B, 8B, 14B), Qwen2.5 (7B, 72B), Mistral-7B and Mistral-Small-24B, Phi-3.5-mini and phi-4, OLMo-2 (1B, 7B, 13B, base and instruct), and Llama-3-8B-Instruct. Three more (Qwen2.5-14B, Mistral-Nemo-12B, Granite-3.3-8B) and two MoE models were used only out of sample.
